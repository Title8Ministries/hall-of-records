The Paper Chains · I — Babylon

Persons as collateral. Debt as a transferable instrument. Time as the measure of human worth.

The Code of Hammurabi, c. 1754 BCE — the first surviving written law on debt, interest rates, banking deposits and receipts, contract formation, and the transfer of persons as collateral for debt. Article 117 allowed a man to pledge his wife, son, or daughter against what he owed for a fixed term of three years. the code — read it

Babylonian temples functioned as the first banks. A merchant could deposit grain or silver, receive a sealed clay tablet as evidence of the deposit, and another person could redeem that tablet at another temple. These were the first negotiable instruments.

When that civilization fell, the paper did not disappear. It moved.

The Paper Chains · II — Rome

The mask was always legally distinct from the breath behind the mask.

Persona Literally a mask, from the Etruscan phersu. In Roman law the persona became the legal identity worn by a human being in the eyes of the law. A single human could wear multiple personas. A persona could exist without any living human behind it at all.
Res A thing — an item of property capable of being owned, possessed, transferred, or destroyed.
Status Your legal standing, which determined what rights and obligations attached to you. The modern concept of standing in American courts descends directly from Roman status.
Caput Literally the head, but legally the sum of one’s status. To suffer capitis deminutio was to lose legal capacity — to be reduced in head.

Roman civil law was compiled under Justinian in 533 CE as the Corpus Juris Civilis. the institutes — read them

When a modern American court treats your name in capital letters as something distinct from you, it is operating on a concept Rome inherited from Babylon and codified for the next two thousand years.

The Paper Chains · III — The Church Holds the Paper

“Porro subesse Romano Pontifici omni humanae creaturae declaramus, dicimus, definimus, et pronunciamus omnino esse de necessitate salutis.”

“We declare, we proclaim, we define that it is absolutely necessary for salvation that every human creature be subject to the Roman Pontiff.”

Boniface VIII, Unam Sanctam, 18 November 1302. Subesse — to be under, to be subject to; a subject is by definition not sovereign. Creatura — a thing created, brought into existence by another. The modern legal phrase “creature of statute” carries exactly this meaning forward. unam sanctam — read it

Dum Diversas · 1452 Authorized the King of Portugal to “invade, search out, capture, vanquish, and subdue” and to “reduce their persons into perpetual slavery.” This is not theology. This is an operational order with legal effect. read the bull
Romanus Pontifex · 1455 Extended these rights specifically to lands and peoples seized along the African coast and beyond. read the bull
Inter Caetera · 1493 Alexander VI literally drew a line down the Atlantic. Continents he had never seen, populated by peoples he had never met, conveyed by a stroke of the pen. read the bull

The religious foundation has been withdrawn at its source. The American cases resting on it have never been overruled.

On 30 March 2023 the Vatican stated that the doctrine of discovery “is not part of the teaching of the Catholic Church,” naming Dum Diversas, Romanus Pontifex and Inter Caetera directly. the 2023 statement johnson v. m’intosh, 21 u.s. 543

The Paper Chains · V — Cestui Que Vie

A living human being and the legal estate associated with that human being are separable entities.

1666 — the year of the Great Fire, in the lingering devastation of a plague that had killed roughly a quarter of the English population. Parliament passed an Act for those absent beyond the seas: after seven years of unexplained absence a person could be declared legally dead and his estate administered by trustees. Cestui que vie is Anglo-Norman French — literally, the one whose life. the act of 1666 — read it

The statute sleeps but is not dead.

The Paper Chains · VI — La Banca

The judge on the bench and the banker behind the counter are operating variations of the same ancient function — the holding and adjudication of paper that represents value.

Banca Italian for bench. The wooden table where moneychangers conducted business in the marketplace.
Banca Rotta Broken bench. The literal breaking of a merchant’s bench in the marketplace when he could not pay his debts.
Banc The raised platform in a courtroom where the judge sits. Same etymological root.
En Banc Literally “on the bench.” When all judges of a court sit together to hear a case.
1927 a judge with “a direct, personal, substantial pecuniary interest in reaching a conclusion against him in his case” denies the defendant due process tumey v. ohio, 273 u.s. 510 1972 the interest does not have to be in his pocket. It only has to be in the institution he is responsible for ward v. village of monroeville, 409 u.s. 57

They are not a general theory that every court is a bank and every case is a bond. They are something much better than a theory: they are two cases you can cite, that have never been overruled, and that a judge has to answer.

The Language Wall · eight definitions of one word

I contend that a word bearing seven meanings is capable of being applied to the wrong one; that where my rights are at stake I am entitled to know which is meant; and that I am entitled to ask.

1 U.S.C. § 1 "the words 'person' and 'whoever' include corporations, companies, associations, firms, partnerships, societies, and joint stock companies, as well as individuals." read § 1
1 U.S.C. § 8(a) "the words 'person', 'human being', 'child', and 'individual', shall include every infant member of the species homo sapiens who is born alive at any stage of development." read § 8
26 U.S.C. § 7701(a)(1) "The term 'person' shall be construed to mean and include an individual, a trust, estate, partnership, association, company or corporation." read § 7701
26 U.S.C. § 7343 "The term 'person' as used in this chapter includes an officer or employee of a corporation, or a member or employee of a partnership, who as such officer, employee, or member is under a duty to perform the act in respect of which the violation occurs." read § 7343
5 U.S.C. § 551(2) "'person' includes an individual, partnership, corporation, association, or public or private organization other than an agency." read § 551
U.C.C. § 1-201(b)(27) "'Person' means an individual, corporation, business trust, estate, trust, partnership, limited liability company, association, joint venture, government, governmental subdivision, agency, or instrumentality, public corporation, or any other legal or commercial entity." read § 1-201
11 U.S.C. § 101(41) "The term 'person' includes individual, partnership, and corporation, but does not include governmental unit" read § 101
15 U.S.C. § 1602(d)-(e) "The term 'person' means a natural person or an organization." read § 1602
